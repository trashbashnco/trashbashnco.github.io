p. 1 The world

Our mess made the great reset.

For as long as we could remember, we bought and consumed without a care. Nothing rotted; it just piled up in our landfills until that wasn't enough either. Governments banded together to mitigate the waste, but it was far too late.

Eventually the waste we neglected seeped into nature itself and nature did the only thing it could: It evolved...

The piles started to move together and the things we discarded grew limbs and thoughts of their own. They were no longer the byproducts of our wallets, no, they were alive.

We called it the great reset. 80% of our marine biology was wiped out within a year. Avians made of umbrellas and mammals carved from produce started popping out around the world.

Now our objects roam the earth, imitating their natural counterparts. This journal was written for the very purpose of understanding this phenomenon and to record what comes next.

The numbers · Singapore

What a nation throws away.

fig. 1 0.83 kg of waste, per person, every day at home. Across Singapore, that's 3,500,000 kg of household waste. Every day. About 300 kg a year each, and that is only what leaves our flats. Factories, shops and offices throw away roughly three times more again. Sources: NEA, Waste Statistics 2025; SingStat, 4.2 million residents
fig. 2 11% of household waste is recycled. Down from about 20% a decade ago. The 2030 target is 30%. The rest is burned, and the ash goes to one island. Source: NEA 2025; MSE
fig. 3 2035 Semakau, our only landfill, is full. Opened 1999. More than half filled. Expected to run out of space by 2035 at current rates. There is no second island. Source: MSE, Jan 2024
The game

You own too much. Let it go.

Six creatures you bought over the years. Each one has a hold on you. Spend Resolve to let them go. First to own nothing wins. Two players, about twenty minutes.
